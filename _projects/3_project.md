What can a song's _lyrics alone_ tell you? This collaborative NLP project set out to answer that
from several angles at once — treating raw lyric text as the only input and using it to predict
commercial performance, emotional tone, and musical genre, then to **generate brand-new lyrics**.

The work was done by a six-person team. My contribution was the **genre classification** model and
the curation of training datasets; the sections below describe the full project, with my piece
called out directly.

## The four analyses

- **Commercial performance prediction** — Engineers features from lyrics (explicit-content
  detection, word metrics, and more) to forecast a song's popularity on a 0–100 scale. The model
  reached an **RMSE of ~23.54**.
- **Emotion classification** — Uses a pretrained **RoBERTa** model to classify the emotional tone
  of a set of lyrics.
- **Genre classification _(my contribution)_** — A **Support Vector Machine** trained on
  **TF-IDF** vectorized lyrics predicts a song's genre. This involved curating a custom lyric
  dataset and tuning text-chunking and train/test configurations to find what the classifier could
  actually learn from words alone.
- **Lyric generation** — A feedforward neural network, trained on the lyric corpus, produces
  original lyrics in the style of its training data.

## From model to music

The project didn't stop at notebooks. The team combined the generative model with original music
composition to release a **complete, produced song** — the music written by a teammate, the lyrics
generated by the model.

🎵 **[Listen to the final track on SoundCloud](https://on.soundcloud.com/fuuW2eBZG72XsSp7A)**

## Tech stack

Python · Jupyter · scikit-learn (SVM) · TF-IDF · RoBERTa · pandas

Datasets were drawn from Kaggle's Spotify audio-and-lyrics collection and an emotion dataset,
supplemented by a custom lyric dataset built to improve model performance.
